**The biggest lever is how accuracy is evaluated, not training.** Training is only 15 h (minimal) to 38 h (full) on an A100. Evaluating gated models token by token at bs=1 is 4–5× the total cost.
- **Why batching is safe:** with fixed calibrated thresholds (D-18), each token's decisions depend only on its own sequence, not on the batch. Accuracy can therefore be measured with batched masked execution, while bs=1 is kept for the latency study (E11). Compute is still counted from the decisions.
- **What it needs:** an equivalence test against the bs=1 engine (task **P5-08**, proposed). Without P5-08, plan on the bs=1 column.

## Assumptions (configs/budget/gpu_budget.yaml)

- **Training run:** 1 epoch of the D-06 mix in chat format, about 17.2k examples at bs=1 (GateSkip's recipe).
  - **A100:** ≈ 0.5 h for Llama-1B and 0.75 h for Qwen-1.5B. That's about 0.1 s/step: ≈ 2e12 FLOPs per step at ≈ 300 tokens, plus Python layer-loop launch overhead and the AdamW step.
  - **4090:** 0.55× A100 training throughput.
  - **Overhead multipliers:** GateSkip +15% (its vector gates are 13.7% of a layer), our method +5%, baselines +10%.
  - **Not measured yet. This is the least certain number: ±2×.** GateSkip's own job allows 2 h per run on an A100 with their code, which we use for E1-official.
- **Seeds:** the full plan uses 3 seeds for every trained method (PROJECT_PLAN). The minimal plan uses 3 seeds only for our main result (E4).
- **Budgets:** one trained model per seed serves all 3 compute budgets through calibration (as GateSkip does), plus a 0.05 h calibration pass. If P3-02's budget schedule ends up needing one model per budget, training hours for our method roughly triple.
- **GSM8K:** 1,319 problems × ≈ 200 generated tokens (our runs: 128–219). MBPP: 500 × 150. ARC-C + PIQA are log-likelihood only (≈ 0.1 h).
- **bs=1 decode, assumed:** HF ≈ 20 ms/token (Llama) and 28 ms/token (Qwen) on either GPU (latency-bound at this size; M4 MPS measured 39 ms/token for Qwen). The gated engine is 1.6× that, the MPS measurement (run `20261106-190740`), and probably less on CUDA. Batched evals are assumed 8× faster.
- **Dev evals** (λ sweep, minimal ablations): 300 problems from the calibration side, never the test set.
- **Not included:** porting/debugging time for FlexiDepth, D-LLM and FFN-skip; failed or repeated runs; Llama access delays; MPS work (free, local). Budget the margin for these.
- **Memory on a 24 GB card:** LoRA (D-13 option b) fits easily. A full fine-tune of Llama-1B in bf16 with fp32 AdamW states (≈ 15 GB + activations) is tight but likely workable. A full fine-tune of Qwen-1.5B (≈ 19 GB + activations) needs 8-bit Adam or gradient checkpointing. D-13 therefore also decides whether a 24 GB card is enough.

## Other GPUs

- **L4 24 GB:** ≈ 0.35× A100 training throughput and ≈ 1.5× the 4090's bs=1 decode time. Roughly 2× the 4090 hours.
- **H100:** ≈ 1.5–2× A100 training throughput; decode at bs=1 is barely faster.

To price a cloud rental, multiply the hours by the provider's on-demand rate for that GPU, plus storage (≈ 100 GB persistent volume for the HF cache, checkpoints and results).
